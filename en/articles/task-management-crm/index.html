What Is CRM Task Management? Scattered Work in One Place

July 25, 2026 · 8 min read

"Who did I give that job to?" "What did this customer last ask for?" "Did we send the quote?" If these questions sound very familiar, the problem is not you; it is that the work is kept in scattered places. Email, WhatsApp, a notebook and memory — none of them is a work tracking system.

What is CRM task management?

CRM task management means tracking every job your team has to do (calls, quotes, visits, follow-ups, deliveries) in one system, with each task linked to the relevant customer record. Every task has an owner, a due date and a customer or opportunity it belongs to, so both "who will do what, by when" and "what happened last with this customer" are answered on the same screen.

A separate to-do list shows the work but not the customer; a CRM on its own shows the customer but does not track who has the job. Put them together and a task appears with the customer's history alongside it:

  • Task: "Send the quote by Friday" — owner: Ayşe.
  • Linked record: the ABC Ltd. opportunity, notes from the two previous conversations and the first quote that was sent.
  • Result: when the task is closed, the next step (calling the customer) opens automatically; no customer is left without an answer.

The real cost of scattered work

When business information is in pieces, it gets lost: the enquiry is in WhatsApp, the related quote is in email, the notes are in someone's notebook, and the follow-up is in nobody's head. The result: forgotten jobs, customers answered late, tasks done twice or not at all. Nobody is lazy; there is simply no system.

What do task management tools solve?

Task management makes the question "who will do what, by when" visible. Tools such as Trello, Asana and ClickUp, or Odoo's Project module, lay work out on boards and lists:

  • Every job is a card, with a clear owner, date and status.
  • The stage each job is at (to do / in progress / done) is visible at a glance.
  • No task disappears because it was "forgotten"; everyone knows their own work.

What does a CRM solve?

A CRM (customer relationship management) collects everything about a customer in one place: who called, what they wanted, which quote was sent, what was last discussed, what the next step is. As a result:

  • Every incoming enquiry (from the website, by phone, from a marketplace) is recorded and none is dropped.
  • The sales pipeline becomes visible: how many opportunities there are, which stage each is at, when it is expected to close.
  • Even if an employee leaves, the customer history stays with the company — in the system, not with the person.
Task management organises the team's work; a CRM organises your relationship with the customer. Put the two together and the "I forgot" excuse disappears.

Combining the two is where the real strength lies

Task management and CRM are each useful on their own; but when they are connected, your workflow becomes a single chain. For example: an enquiry comes in from the website → it lands in the CRM as an opportunity → a task is assigned to the sales team → a quote is prepared → once it is approved, a task is opened for the production or project team. The enquiry flows through one system from first contact to delivery, without getting lost.

In an integrated system such as Odoo, CRM, tasks, quoting and invoicing talk to each other in the same place, so building this chain is much easier. Instead of five separate apps, everything is connected in one system.

How to set up task management in a CRM: 5 steps

  1. Write down the stages of the work. Define the stages of your customer work, such as enquiry, quote, order, delivery and payment; the sales pipeline in the CRM is built from these.
  2. Define the tasks for each stage. For example, at the "quote" stage: take measurements, calculate the price, send the quote, call two days later.
  3. Set the owner and the deadline up front. Every task should have an owner and a due date; an overdue task should land on the manager's screen.
  4. Take every enquiry through one door. Every enquiry from the website, phone or WhatsApp should go into the CRM as a record, and tasks should be opened from that record.
  5. Look at the same screen once a week. Overdue tasks, unanswered customers and opportunities about to close should be the agenda of the weekly meeting.

You do not need a big system to start: beginning with one sales pipeline and a few task templates, then expanding as the team gets used to it, is the path that works most often.

Frequently asked questions

Are CRM and task management the same thing?

No. Task management tracks the team's work (who, what, by when); a CRM tracks the relationship with the customer (who called, what they asked for, which quote went out). CRM task management combines the two: every task is linked to a customer record.

We use Trello or Asana — do we need a CRM as well?

If your tasks are about customers, usually yes. Trello and Asana show the work, but they do not keep customer history, quotes or the sales pipeline; information is carried between the two tools by hand and gets lost. If customer work is the bulk of what you do, keeping tasks inside the CRM means less gets dropped.

Is CRM task management overkill for a small team?

No; it helps small teams most, because there the business knowledge sits in a few people's heads and disappears when one of them is on leave. For a team of three to five, one sales pipeline and a few task templates are enough.

Which software can you use?

Odoo, HubSpot, Zoho CRM and Pipedrive can all link tasks to a customer record. If you want quotes, stock and invoicing in the same system too, an integrated system such as Odoo lets a task move from sale to delivery without breaking the chain.
