From Excel to ERP: why spreadsheets stop being enough as your business grows

July 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Almost every company starts out with an Excel file. The problem is not Excel; it is that as the business grows, the spreadsheet ends up carrying a load it was never meant to bear. Noticing that threshold is the decision most businesses make too late, and at the highest cost.

With a small team, a handful of customers and a single spreadsheet, everything works. Quotes are on one tab, stock on another, customer and supplier balances in a third file. Everybody knows where everything is, because "everybody" is two or three people.

Then the business grows. And growth is where Excel quietly starts to break down.

The point where spreadsheets break: 5 signs

If three of the following sound familiar, the spreadsheets are no longer running your business; you are running the spreadsheets:

  • You enter the same data in more than one place. The order is in one file, the shipment in another, the invoice in a third — and all three are updated by hand.
  • The "which one is current?" question. Copies called "final_v3_real_final" are going around and nobody knows which one to trust.
  • Producing a report takes a whole day. For month-end profit or the value of open quotes, somebody has to sit down and merge files for hours.
  • The knowledge is in one person's head. When that person goes on leave, work stops.
  • Mistakes turn into money. A price copied wrongly, a shipment forgotten, a receivable overlooked.
Excel is a calculation tool, not a system. What you need as the company grows is not calculation but a single, reliable source of truth.

So why does everyone still put it off?

Because the move sounds big, expensive and risky. "We're managing for now" is a comforting sentence until the cost of the breakdown becomes visible. Yet the real cost builds up in the small amounts of time lost every day and in the mistakes nobody notices.

The second fear is licence cost. The word "ERP" brings high annual licence fees to mind. That is no longer unavoidable: on an open-source core such as Odoo Community, you can set up a full business system without paying for licences.

What does a good transition look like?

Moving to ERP does not mean "changing everything overnight". A good transition happens in phases:

1. Start where it hurts most

Trying to move the whole company at once is the most common mistake. First get the process that wears you down most — usually quoting or inventory — working properly. See a concrete gain.

2. Migrate your existing data; do not start from zero

The customer, product and price information you have built up over the years is valuable. In a well-planned transition that history is preserved.

3. Fit the system to your business, not your business to the system

Do not let an off-the-shelf package force you into its mould. A good implementation learns the way you work and takes shape around it.

What do you gain?

  • Single data entry — information is entered once and is up to date everywhere.
  • Instant reports — profit, open quotes and receivables on one screen.
  • Company memory — the knowledge lives in the system, not in individuals.
  • Scalability — a team of 3 and a team of 30 use the same system.

If Excel has brought you this far, it has done its job well. But what will carry you from here is a system, not a spreadsheet.
